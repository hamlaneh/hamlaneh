Message #deploys
The idle state — same strip, same slot
No call in this channelWhoever joins first opens the roomStart a call
One strip with two states, not two things. A room is created by whoever joins first, so starting and joining are the same act with two labels — and because the idle state is always present, going live changes the strip’s content and never the layout. Nothing shoves the message list.
Entry, and not annoying
The slot is permanent at 60 px, so a call starting mid-read moves nothing. The transition is a 180 ms opacity and 2 px rise on the strip’s own contents, within existing durations.
It never steals focus, never animates position, and carries no sound. This is the only call surface a person meets without having chosen a call, so it stays a strip and never becomes a dialog.
Under prefers-reduced-motion it is a single-frame swap.
At ≤899 the avatar row drops and the names truncate to a count; the action keeps its 44 px height and full label.

What it deliberately does not wear
No AuthShell, no AuthForm, no PrimaryButton. That is the sign-in treatment and wearing it is how a page starts implying an account.
No centred card on a tinted ground, no product wordmark lockup, no "or continue with" rule, no link to sign-in or to registration anywhere on the page.

The field is autocomplete="name" and never username or email — a password manager offering to save credentials here would say the opposite of the copy.

One sentence, three causesUnknown, expired and revoked all answer with a single 404 and the page cannot tell them apart. Naming a cause would be invention, and a wrong one sends the visitor to do the wrong thing — "wait for it to be reissued" for a link somebody deliberately killed. It is also what the contract protects: a distinct refusal for revoked would confirm the conference exists. Same shape, same reasoning as the invite redemption screen’s unusable state.
Where the way out goesTo the instance’s front door, not to sign-in. A stranger has no account here, so offering one answers a question they did not ask. The identity line stays in the rule — they still deserve to know whose link failed — but the "is hosting this meeting" clause drops, because there is no meeting to host. The language switcher stays, because a person who cannot read the sentence cannot act on it. Unreachable is a different state: "This meeting could not be checked" with a retry, never merged into this one, because it is not a claim about the link.

پیام به #deploys
Control-bar orderThe bar is one logical row and mirrors whole: the three toggles sit at logical inline-start — the right in Persian — then the rule, then the participant count. Leave stays at logical inline-end, so in Persian it is on the left. It keeps the same 22 px gap and its own rule, so the distance that protects it from a mis-click is a logical distance and survives the mirror. DOM, reading and tab order are unchanged; nothing is reversed to imitate the visual order.
Share-rail sideThe face rail sits at logical inline-end, which is the left in Persian. The shared screen keeps the logical inline-start, because the share is the subject and the subject leads. At ≤899 the rail becomes a horizontal strip above the control bar in both directions, and the strip scrolls along the inline axis.
Active-speaker emphasisDirection-neutral by construction: a 2 px inset ring on all four sides plus name weight 600. The level meter is the one directional part — its bars fill from logical inline-start, so in Persian they grow rightward, and it sits beside the name at the plate’s inline-end. Emphasis is never carried by colour alone.
Glyph mirroring — opt-in, per icon
Nothing in this set mirrors. Microphone, microphone-off, video, video-off, monitor-up, users and phone-off are all non-directional — an object or a state, not a direction — and the slash on the two off-glyphs is a fixed diagonal that would read as a different mark reversed.
The only mirroring glyphs in the whole package remain arrow-left and log-out, neither of which appears on a call surface. arrow-right on the guest page mirrors, being the same directional family.
Numerals and bidi
Every app-generated number is ASCII 0–9 — the participant count in the bar, timestamps, the grid annotation. This is the locked package rule, not a per-screen choice.
The complete slug renders as one isolated LTR unit, <bdi dir="ltr">#deploys</bdi>, so it never reads as deploys#.
Display names are dir="auto" runs, so Omid Rezaei stays Latin inside a Persian call and no name is transliterated.
Vazirmatn at the existing Persian line-height, no negative tracking, no new type step.

Grid at 2, 3, 5 and 12 — and beyond
2 — two cells side by side, one row, each tile filling half the stage. 3 — two columns; the third tile sits in the second row at single-column width, flush to the logical inline-start, rather than stretching across.
5 — three columns, two rows, six cells with the last left empty. The grid keeps whole rows instead of resizing tiles to fill, so a person joining never resizes everybody.
12 — four columns, three rows, all cells used. This is the densest layout drawn.
Beyond 12 — the grid stops growing. Eleven cells carry the most recent speakers, and the twelfth becomes a count cell reading +8 more with their names beneath it. It is a count, not a participant tile: no avatar, no plate, not focusable as a person.
Phone width is a separate layout, not a shrink. At ≤899 the stage becomes a single column of 16:9 tiles that scrolls, the active speaker pinned to the top, and the control bar becomes a fixed bottom row at 56 px with the same five controls at full size.
Leave, and where the call lives
Leave ends the call for the person clicking and for nobody else. There is no end-for-everyone anywhere in this set, because nothing in the product grants it.
It is a solid danger fill with the phone-off glyph and the full words Leave call — unmistakable at a glance.
It sits at the logical inline-end of the bar, behind a 22 px gap and its own vertical rule, with the three toggles clustered at the opposite end. A hand reaching for mute cannot arrive at it, and the protecting distance is logical, so it survives the Persian mirror.
The call is a sibling of the message column, never an overlay. It takes the upper region of the channel pane and the conversation continues beneath it — people talk and type at once, and nothing is hidden behind the call.
Navigating to another channel does not end the session: the call collapses to the banner strip carrying Return to call, so a call somebody navigated away from is always leavable. That is the whole reason it is not an overlay.
